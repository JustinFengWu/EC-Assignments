We have to unerstand the the problem settings once again. For MaxCoverage and MaxInfluence 
each problem involves maximizing fitness while being under a uniform constraint, and also 
minimise cost. This problem setting would turn these problems into exploring a pareto front
of trade-offs between slution quality and resources usage.

Design Choices:

Binary representation of solutions. Implmentation and reasoning exactly the same as single-
-objective.

Population initialisation being the same as SOE, with population size being 50.

Fitness evaluation is returned by the problem instance.

The mutation operation is still bit-flip with flipping chance of 1/n.

The repair logic is the same with SOE too. Infeasible solutions of negative fitness values
are thrown out.

Parent selection is done through tournament selection, and random selection for non-dominated
individuals.
Each iteration creates one child per parent.

Diversity and ranking is the key differnence to SOE.
To balance cost and reward, non-dominated sorting and crowding distance was used following
the NSGA-II principle.
- classify population into pareto fronts F1, F2, ... where F1 are the non-dominated solutions.
- Lower Rank fronts have higher priority for survival.
Crowding distance:
- within each front, estimate density of neighbours, and prefer more isolated individuals as
it can help maintain diversity.
Survivor Selection:
- Sort by Dominance Rank, where within each front, by descending crowding distance. 

Design Choice Reasoning:
Fast Non-dominated sorting was chosen to be implemented as it maintian pareto fronts very well
and it is proven on submodular problems.

corwding distance preserves the spread of solutions, which encourages variety in reward vs cost.

population size of 50 was chosen as it functioned best after experimenting with 10, 20, and 50

Repair mechanism used again with reasoning simlar to that of SOE's, which is to keep population
feasbile without penalties that are way too harsh.
